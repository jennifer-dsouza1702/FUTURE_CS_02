# FUTURE_CS_02  
## Phishing Email Detection & Awareness Report

---

## Overview
This project was completed as part of my Cyber Security Internship at Future Interns.

The objective was to analyze real-world phishing email scenarios and create an **awareness-focused security report** that helps users identify and avoid phishing attacks.

Unlike technical exploitation tasks, this project focuses on **human-centered security**, understanding how attackers manipulate users rather than systems.

---

## Objectives
- Analyze phishing email samples  
- Identify common phishing indicators  
- Understand how attacks are designed  
- Classify email risks  
- Create user-friendly awareness guidelines  

---

## Email Samples Analyzed

This report includes analysis of **3 realistic phishing scenarios**:

### Example 1: LinkedIn Password Reset Scam
- Spoofed sender domain  
- Malicious “Cancel Reset” link  
- Use of real PIN to appear legitimate  

**Attack Goal:** Credential theft via fake login page  

---

### Example 2: Bank of America Alert
- Fake sender domain  
- Fear-based urgency (“account on hold”)  
- Generic login link  

**Attack Goal:** Capture banking credentials  

---

### Example 3: Facebook Security Update Scam
- Fake breach claim (“50 million accounts”)  
- Malicious password reset link  
- Fake authority figure  

**Attack Goal:** Account takeover  

---

## Key Phishing Indicators

Across all emails, common patterns were identified:

- **Spoofed sender domains** (fake email addresses)  
- **Urgency & fear tactics** (“act now”, “account locked”)  
- **Credential harvesting links**  
- **Use of real names for personalization**  
- **Fake authority & unverifiable claims**  
- **Typos or inconsistencies**  

---

## Risk Classification
All analyzed emails were classified as:

> **PHISHING (High Risk)**  

These attacks can lead to:
- Account compromise  
- Financial loss  
- Identity theft  

---

## Methodology
- Collected phishing email samples  
- Analyzed sender domains and structure  
- Inspected links and redirection behavior  
- Identified psychological manipulation techniques  
- Documented findings in a structured format  

---

## Key Learnings
- Phishing attacks target **human behavior, not just systems**  
- Urgency and trust are the most exploited factors  
- Even well-designed emails can be malicious  
- Awareness is one of the strongest defenses  

---

## Prevention Guidelines

### Do’s
- Check the full sender email address  
- Hover over links before clicking  
- Visit websites directly instead of using email links  
- Enable two-factor authentication  
- Report suspicious emails immediately  

### Don’ts
- Don’t trust emails based on appearance alone  
- Don’t click urgent “reset” or “verify” links  
- Don’t enter credentials from email redirects  
- Don’t ignore suspicious activity  

---
## Conclusion
This project highlights that cybersecurity is not only about protecting systems, but also about **educating users**.

The most important defense against phishing is simple:

> **Pause. Think. Verify.**

A few seconds of awareness can prevent major security incidents.
